What it’s doing

The finder is the verification

Operators pick their restaurant while typing. Junk and blank names disappear on their own, and “not listed yet” is a visible option that pre-selects brand-new opening. The lookup only ever sees the name field — it never touches contact details, and it never blocks a submit.

The traps are invisible

A field humans never see, plus a three-second floor on how fast anyone could really fill this in. Either one trips and the visitor gets the same polite thank-you while nothing is created. No CAPTCHA to squint at on a phone.

The dropdown does the hard part

Meta pre-fills every text input, so accidental submitters sail through anything you can type into. A required business type with no “Other” is the one field that demands a real decision — and where non-restaurants exit.

Nothing gets blocked

gmial.com.com gets an inline fix before submit, but it only suggests. Free inboxes are welcome: requiring a business email would have turned away Just Wing It and Tautog Tavern. The domain feeds scoring, never a gate.

Four free checks ride along, none of them gate

Whether the email domain matches the restaurant’s own website (match / personal / different) — a personal inbox is its own answer, not a failure. Whether the phone is the restaurant’s listed line. Whether the address is a throwaway. And whether Google has the place marked closed — which pre-selects “brand-new opening,” because a shuttered listing usually means someone is taking the space over.
